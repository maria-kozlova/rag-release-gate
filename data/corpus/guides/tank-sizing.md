# Tank and Enclosure Sizing Guide

Minimum volumes below are floor requirements for a healthy adult animal, not
targets. Every figure assumes the filtration, heating and cover appropriate to
the species. Where a species is listed with a footprint rather than a volume,
the footprint is what matters and a taller tank of the same footprint buys you
nothing.

## How we measure a minimum

A minimum volume at Tidepool & Tail means the smallest enclosure in which an
adult of that species can express normal behaviour — swimming, burrowing,
climbing or basking — for its full life, without a planned upgrade.

We measure aquatic volume as **net water volume**: the tank's nominal rating
minus substrate, hardscape and the gap below the rim. A nominal 20-gallon tank
with a 3-inch substrate bed and a planted hardscape holds closer to 15 gallons
of water, and that is the number the stocking rules use.

For terrestrial species we measure usable floor area in square feet and, for
burrowers, substrate depth in inches. Vertical space counts only for arboreal
species.

## Coldwater aquatic species

- **Pearl-scale axolotl** — 20 gallons net for one adult, and 10 gallons for
  each additional animal. The footprint matters more than the depth; a long
  tank beats a tall one of the same volume. Water must stay below 68 °F, which
  in most homes means a chiller rather than a fan.
- **Verdant ribbon loach** — 30 gallons net, kept in groups of three or more. A
  solitary ribbon loach stops feeding within a fortnight and does not recover
  reliably once it starts.

Coldwater species do not share a tank with tropical species. The temperature
bands do not overlap, and running a compromise temperature stresses both.

## Tropical community species

- **Copperfin rasbora** — 15 gallons net for a school of eight, which is the
  minimum viable school. Add 1 gallon per fish beyond eight.
- **Moonlace shrimp** — 5 gallons net for a colony of ten. Shrimp colonies
  self-limit to the available biofilm, so a larger tank produces a larger
  colony rather than healthier individuals.
- **Halcyon betta** — 5 gallons net for a single animal. Halcyon bettas are
  more tolerant of tankmates than most bettas but still may not be housed
  together.

## Arboreal and terrestrial reptiles

- **Banded dune gecko** — 20 gallons, measured as floor footprint. This is a
  ground-dwelling species and added height is wasted. Provide a basking
  gradient from 78 °F to 92 °F along the long axis.
- **Ashwood tree skink** — 40 gallons in a vertical orientation, with a minimum
  interior height of 24 inches and at least two full-height climbing routes. A
  40-gallon tank laid on its long side does not satisfy this.
- **Sablewing anole** — 18 gallons vertical for one adult. Sablewing anoles are
  territorial past six months and are housed singly unless the enclosure
  exceeds 40 gallons with visual breaks.

## Burrowing small mammals

Volume is the wrong measure here; depth and floor area are the constraints.

- **Cloudtail jerboa** — 4 square feet of floor and at least **10 inches** of
  packable burrowing substrate. Jerboas kept on shallow bedding pace and
  bar-chew, and wire flooring causes sores within weeks.
- **Marbled dormouse** — 3 square feet of floor plus vertical branch routes.
  Dormice are semi-arboreal and use height that jerboas ignore.
- **Sootfoot gerbil** — 2.5 square feet of floor and 6 inches of substrate for
  a bonded pair.

## When to upgrade

Upgrade when the animal reaches adult size, when a group grows, or when water
parameters stop holding between weekly changes. A tank that needs more than one
water change a week to stay stable is undersized for its stock regardless of
what the table above says.
