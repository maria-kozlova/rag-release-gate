# Species Compatibility Guide

Compatibility is not a property of a pair of species. It is a property of a
pair of species in a particular enclosure, at a particular temperature, at a
particular life stage. Two animals that coexist in a 55-gallon planted tank may
not coexist in a bare 20.

## The three rules

Every pairing we recommend satisfies all three of the following. A pairing that
fails any one of them is not a pairing we will ship together.

1. **Overlapping temperature band.** The two species must share a range of at
   least 4 °F where both are comfortable, not merely alive.
2. **Non-overlapping territory.** Two species that use the same layer of the
   tank or enclosure — same substrate, same branch height, same basking spot —
   will compete even when neither is aggressive.
3. **Comparable size at adulthood.** Anything that fits in another animal's
   mouth eventually goes in it. We use adult size, not size at sale.

## Aquatic pairings

**Recommended.**

- Copperfin rasbora with Moonlace shrimp, in a planted tank of 15 gallons net
  or larger. Rasboras occupy the mid-water column, shrimp work the substrate
  and hardscape, and adult shrimp are too large for a rasbora to take.
- Verdant ribbon loach with Copperfin rasbora, 30 gallons net or larger. Both
  are tropical, and the loach stays on the bottom.
- Halcyon betta with Moonlace shrimp, 10 gallons net or larger, with dense
  planting. This one is conditional: individual bettas vary, and a betta that
  hunts shrimp will not stop.

**Not recommended.**

- Pearl-scale axolotl with anything. Axolotls are kept in a species-only tank.
  They are coldwater, they take anything that fits in the mouth, and small
  tankmates nip their gills.
- Halcyon betta with Halcyon betta, at any volume.
- Verdant ribbon loach kept alone. This is a stocking failure rather than a
  compatibility failure, but it is the most common one we see.

## Reptile cohabitation

We do not recommend mixed-species reptile enclosures at any size. The
temperature, humidity and substrate requirements below are close enough to look
compatible and far enough apart to cause chronic stress.

- **Banded dune gecko** — 78 °F to 92 °F gradient, 30% humidity, sand-free
  substrate for animals under six months.
- **Ashwood tree skink** — 72 °F to 84 °F, 65% humidity, deep leaf litter.
- **Sablewing anole** — 74 °F to 86 °F, 70% humidity, vertical planting.

Same-species groups are possible for the dune gecko and the tree skink in
enclosures above 40 gallons with duplicated basking sites. Sablewing anoles are
housed singly past six months regardless of enclosure size.

## Small-mammal grouping

- **Cloudtail jerboa** — solitary. Jerboas introduced as adults fight, and the
  fights are usually fatal for one of them.
- **Marbled dormouse** — same-sex pairs or trios, introduced before four months
  of age. Adult introductions fail more often than they succeed.
- **Sootfoot gerbil** — bonded pairs, always. A single gerbil is an animal
  welfare problem, and a broken bond cannot be re-formed between the same two
  animals.

Never house two species of small mammal in one habitat. Scent marking alone is
enough to keep both animals in a permanent stress state.

## Quarantine

Every new animal is quarantined for **21 days** in a separate enclosure with its
own equipment before it joins an established group or tank. This applies to
animals bought from us, and it applies even where the animal looks perfectly
healthy on arrival — most of what quarantine catches is asymptomatic on day one.

Quarantine equipment is never shared. A net, siphon or feeding tong that has
been in a quarantine tank does not go back into a display tank without a full
disinfection cycle.

## Known incompatibilities worth repeating

Coldwater and tropical species never share water. Burrowing and arboreal small
mammals never share a habitat. Reptiles never share an enclosure across species.
These three lines account for nearly every compatibility question we receive.
